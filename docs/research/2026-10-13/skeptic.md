# Evidence and usability challenge

13 October 2026 · reviewer role: evidence/usability skeptic · agent `/root/evidence_skeptic`

Status: desk research and adversarial design review. No Flyji customers were interviewed or observed. The requirements already confirmed by the owner remain constraints: serve travelers, enthusiasts and researchers; retain all three visual directions for refinement; use concise plain copy; explore useful spatial and temporal interactions. This review challenges the proposed ways of satisfying those requirements. It does not approve a final design or public launch.

## Review basis

Read [customer inventory](../../design/customer-needs.md), [exploration program](../../design/exploration-program.md), and [R08 product record](../../product/README.md). These correctly label customer needs as hypotheses and current QA as author testing. Product capabilities described below are statements in that record, not independently rerun implementation checks by this agent. Technical QA ownership belongs to the audit role.

The strongest unresolved question is whether Flyji helps a person make a useful decision with the facts it has. A larger interface inventory and agent agreement cannot answer it. A traveler may need price, baggage, bookability and protected transfers before a schedule matters. An enthusiast may value geographic discovery. A researcher may reject attractive output without a defensible denominator. Keep all three needs visible; verify their value separately.

## Comparative testing without steering

Preserve the owner's three visual directions. A useful comparison can vary one interaction mechanism at a time inside them: compact list/date-first comparison; linked map with aligned time lanes; optional globe with the same exact facts and names. A topology view is an additional geography experiment, not a fourth approved visual direction. Use the same snapshots, selectable IDs, timing truth and error conditions. Presentation differences cannot receive richer data or easier fixtures.

Use realistic tasks describing an outcome, not instructions such as “rotate the globe” or “use the builder.” Rotate presentation order and use matched tasks with different answers to reduce rehearsal. Record assistance and prompting. Interpretation and successful decisions matter alongside time; a quick wrong answer is a failure. A qualitative round cannot substantiate a numerical winning rate for the entire population.

Keep task domains separate: a globe could aid antimeridian/polar orientation while adding effort to a meeting-deadline choice. A beautiful display can earn an optional place without winning every task. A simple list also needs evaluation; plainness is not evidence of usefulness.

## Round-one exchange with the customer advocate

The advocate and skeptic independently raised the schedule-only value risk, regional variation, unknown data, transfer semantics and drag alternatives. These are evidence/protocol agreements, not customer validation.

On challenge, the advocate accepted that mode-first entry and globe default are unproven. The advocate proposed a concrete globe hypothesis: orientation of a dated antimeridian/polar route without a flattened seam, explicitly comparing a flat map, topology and named leg list. The advocate does not claim the globe uniquely solves this problem. The skeptic accepts that as a legitimate experiment, while retaining the occlusion, density and navigation objections in D01.

The advocate also conceded that schedule-only repeat value is a hypothesis for expert construction, enthusiast network inspection and reproducible research; traveler repeat value is especially uncertain without comparison to price-first tools. D04 remains open. Agreement about uncertainty is not approval to publish a travel planner that appears to answer unsupported commercial or transfer questions.

## Round-two exchange and changed position

The skeptic directly challenged five points: mode-first entry; rare geography tasks as justification for a globe default; uncertainties hidden until detail; feature inventory turning into implementation breadth; and nine initial sessions being treated as broad validation.

The advocate defended three task/action entries as one candidate alongside a compact universal query and contextual actions. The advocate would drop mode-first where first-use people misclassify their task, need coaching, or lose/re-enter intent across tasks. The skeptic accepts this comparative plan. Entry labels and final navigation remain pending.

The advocate disputed an unquestioned universal list-first default. The skeptic changed position: readable lists/lanes have a stronger initial rationale for known-trip deadlines and connection comparisons, but that does not establish a default for geography exploration or service research. Both roles agree to test ordinary geography and rare polar/date-line tasks separately, and retain representation choices per task when outcomes differ. The globe remains a legitimate optional candidate, with hidden options, recovery, tracking misbelief and phone cost as explicit rejection evidence. Neither a globe-first nor a list-first global mandate is agreed.

On commercial and transfer information, the advocate argued that repeating every unknown on every card could make people stop reading. The skeptic accepts that counterargument as a hypothesis to test. Compare concise scope at the choice area plus candidate-specific airport-change/transfer markers with per-card markers. Relevant assumptions and uncertainty must be visible before selection; full detail can be on demand. The failure condition is a mistaken belief that the candidate is purchasable/protected or the transfer is certified, rather than inability to recite a warning. No copy pattern is declared successful yet.

Both roles agree that the wide inventory remains research, with a few named value/risk questions tested before expanding implementation. The advocate revised the historical nine-session suggestion into per-question inclusive rounds with explicit task/context coverage, additional recruitment for unresolved questions, and task-specific designs where outcomes conflict. Nine sessions are neither an exit condition nor evidence for unattempted tasks.

Scoped agreement: three entry candidates; matched facts across preserved visual directions; task-specific geographic/temporal/evidence comparisons; decision-point uncertainty; per-question inclusive rounds; no implementation of the entire inventory by default. Remaining dissent: traveler repeat value, mode/default choice, actual connection suitability and final visual direction are not established. This is agreement on how to investigate the product, with human evidence and owner decisions still pending.
