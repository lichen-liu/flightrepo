# Evidence and interpretation

Retrieved 13 October 2026. Primary sources support the narrow claims below. Public survey populations, product patterns, airport instructions and standards are different evidence types; none is an observed Flyji customer session. No public statistics are converted into invented Flyji weights.

## E01 · CAA 2025 Aviation Consumer Survey, Wave 14

Source: [CAA 2025 Aviation Consumer Survey, Wave 14](https://www.caa.co.uk/publication/download/26850). Type: `external_survey`. Locator: Q7 slide34/PDFpage33; methodology slide61/PDFpage60; publication CAP3213 dated26Feb2026.

In October 2025, 3,500 UK adults were surveyed. Q7 reports top-three aviation-industry investment priorities: reducing cost 52%, airport queues 41%, disruption 36%, environmental impact 32%, ground access 29%.

Limits: Industry-investment priorities, not flight-choice weights, Flyji preferences, or a global ranking. Self-report; the detailed top-three question takes precedence over the executive summary. Search metadata incorrectly labels the PDF Wave13/2024; body and publication identify Wave14/2025.

## E02 · IATA 2025 Global Passenger Survey release

Source: [IATA 2025 Global Passenger Survey release](https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2025-releases/2025-11-05-02/). Type: `external_survey_summary`. Locator: Mobile reliance, regional trends, About GPS.

The release reports over10,000 responses from more than200 countries, growing phone use and regional differences, including North American emphasis on journey duration and fewer layovers.

Limits: Industry-published summary; full sampling/weighting details not audited here. No inference of Flyji audience size, causal behavior, or one universal priority.

## E03 · Google Flights official help

Source: [Google Flights official help](https://support.google.com/travel/answer/2475306?hl=en). Type: `product_documentation`. Locator: Find flights; flight insights; self-transfer tickets; missing flights.

Documents city/airport input, date calendar, map discovery, stops/airline/time filters and booking handoff. It describes baggage and missed-onward-flight issues for separate tickets.

Limits: A product pattern and commercial context; no evidence that Flyji users prefer this interface or that any Flyji journey is purchasable.

## E04 · Heathrow connecting flights guidance

Source: [Heathrow connecting flights guidance](https://www.heathrow.com/connecting-flights). Type: `airport_guidance`. Locator: Separate tickets; transfer questions.

Transfer procedure depends on the booking and journey; separate tickets can require immigration, baggage collection/recheck and security.

Limits: Heathrow-specific contextual guidance. A city relation or generic elapsed gap cannot establish feasibility. Current instructions must be checked for the actual trip.

## E05 · WCAG2.2 W3C Recommendation

Source: [WCAG2.2 W3C Recommendation](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/). Type: `standard`. Locator: 2.1.1;2.5.7;2.5.8;1.4.10;4.1.3.

Contains keyboard, non-drag pointer access, minimum target-size, reflow and status-message criteria.

Limits: Using these criteria as requirements does not establish compliance. Full processes and human/assistive-technology evaluation remain necessary.

## E06 · GOV.UK planning user research rounds

Source: [GOV.UK planning user research rounds](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/plan-round-of-user-research). Type: `method_guidance`. Locator: Objectives; participants; prepare prototype; practice session.

Research rounds need explicit objectives, suitable participants/access needs, informed consent, working prototypes and preparation.

Limits: Method guidance, not an aviation preference study or evidence that the proposed sample is sufficient.

## E07 · GOV.UK planning research for a service

Source: [GOV.UK planning research for a service](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/plan-user-research-for-your-service). Type: `method_guidance`. Locator: Research questions; rounds; participant numbers.

Turn unsupported assumptions into research questions and adjust rounds as findings emerge; qualitative rounds and quantitative surveys require different sample planning.

Limits: No universal nine-person saturation threshold. Flyji recruitment, sessions and population estimates are still absent.

## Owner and code evidence

O01–O08 in the register capture explicit session requirements; they establish product-owner direction, not market demand. O08 puts exploration, selection, analysis and reversible play at the center of the next slice. R08 implementation status comes from its retained source/QA records and current source inspection. Important correction: the CAA detailed question counts placement among three priorities. We reject the interpretation that 52% ranked cost first or that those percentages are flight-selection weights. The metadata/body mismatch is recorded rather than silently inheriting the search title.
